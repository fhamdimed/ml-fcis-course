Practical Lab Overview

The practical labs are designed to turn Machine Learning concepts into observable model behavior and evidence-based decisions.

You will not only run algorithms. You will also formulate learning problems, inspect and prepare data, predict model behavior before execution, perform important calculations manually, compare alternative models and hyperparameters, diagnose errors, evaluate generalization, and explain why particular results occur.

Across the semester, the labs progress from Machine Learning foundations and data preparation to regression, classification, model evaluation, decision trees, K-Nearest Neighbors, neural networks, clustering, and dimensionality reduction.

ImportantWhat matters in the labs

A correct output or high model score is not enough.

You should be able to explain what the model learned, why a particular preprocessing or modeling decision was made, how the result was evaluated, and what would change if the data, representation, parameter, threshold, or model changed.

How the Labs Work

A typical two-hour lab follows this progression:

  1. Objectives and recap — connect the practical activity to the lecture concepts.
  2. Prediction before execution — state what you expect before running the code.
  3. Guided demonstration — examine a small worked example or manual computation.
  4. Core practical task — implement or complete the main Machine Learning activity.
  5. Experiment and analysis — change data, preprocessing, model settings, thresholds, or hyperparameters and interpret the effect.
  6. Challenge or debugging task — identify, repair, or explain an incorrect workflow.
  7. Reflection and submission — summarize the most important observation and evidence.

The exact timing may vary by lab, but the emphasis remains the same: implementation should lead to reasoning, experimentation, and interpretation.

Lab Assessment

Lab Experiments contribute 10 marks to the course assessment.

Evidence of understanding may include:

  • correct implementation;
  • algorithmic or modeling justification;
  • experimental analysis;
  • manual calculations, traces, and predictions;
  • preprocessing and leakage reasoning;
  • debugging;
  • interpretation of model behavior and metrics;
  • short individual understanding checks;
  • complete and readable submissions.

A working notebook can earn only part of the available credit if the underlying Machine Learning concepts cannot be explained.

NoteBe ready to explain your work

You may be asked to predict what will happen after a small change, explain a selected part of your code, reproduce an important calculation, interpret an unexpected metric, or justify why a particular preprocessing, model, threshold, or hyperparameter was chosen.

Practical Tools

The main environment is:

  • Python 3
  • Jupyter Notebook
  • NumPy
  • Pandas
  • Matplotlib
  • SciPy where statistical analysis is required
  • scikit-learn

Some core computations are implemented manually before using library versions so that the underlying algorithmic ideas remain visible.

Libraries are then used to build reproducible workflows and to focus on model behavior, evaluation, generalization, and interpretation.

NotePractical Reminder

The purpose of the labs is not to produce the longest notebook or the highest score on the training data.

Aim for a small, correct, reproducible Machine Learning workflow that you can explain, evaluate, and defend.
